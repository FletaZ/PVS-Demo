PVS: Psychoacoustic Voice Shield for Proactive Protection Against Voice Cloning

Yiwei Hu, Kuntharrgyal Khysru, Wenjie Tang, Jianguo Wei

Abstract

Voice cloning based on zero-shot TTS/VC models makes it easy to imitate a target speaker from a short reference recording. This paper proposes Psychoacoustic Voice Shield (PVS), a proactive protection framework that injects carefully crafted perturbations into the user’s reference speech so that human listeners still perceive high-quality audio, while reducing the ability of downstream TTS/VC systems to preserve the true speaker identity. PVS operates in the short-time Fourier transform (STFT) magnitude domain and combines a Nesterov-accelerated iterative FGSM scheme that minimizes speaker-embedding similarity with a loudness-normalized psychoacoustic masking loss that confines perturbation energy to perceptually masked time–frequency regions. Consequently, the structured perturbation regulates the trade-off between attack strength and perceptual quality via a single weight parameter. Experiments on multiple personalized TTS/VC models and several ASV systems show that, under the same perturbation budget, PVS achieves stronger or comparable speaker obfuscation than gradient-based baselines while substantially improving objective quality and imperceptibility, and maintains comparable cross-model transferability while improving perceptual quality under the evaluated settings.

Demo Settings

This demo uses XTTS v2 as the voice cloning model. The examples are selected from the LibriSpeech test-clean dataset using a fixed random seed, maintaining a gender balance across 4 speakers (2 males, 2 females). The text content of the cloned speech is consistent with the transcript of the reference speech.
